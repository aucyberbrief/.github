# AU Cyber Brief

Short daily cyber security briefings with an Australian focus, published each weekday morning, and
periodic Intel reports on the patterns across them.

## What we publish

| | What | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 📰 | **Daily brief**: the day's notable incidents, vulnerabilities, advisories, research and law-enforcement action, Australia first | [`briefings/daily/`](https://github.com/aucyberbrief/brief-public/tree/main/briefings/daily) |
| 📊 | **Intel reports**: monthly, quarterly and yearly pattern analysis across the briefs, as Markdown and PDF | [`intel/`](https://github.com/aucyberbrief/brief-public/tree/main/intel) |

Everything is in [**brief-public**](https://github.com/aucyberbrief/brief-public), and all of it is `TLP:CLEAR`.

## How the briefs are written

- **Australia first.** Each brief leads with what affects Australian organisations, then what matters most to
  Australian defenders worldwide: actively exploited vulnerabilities, major incidents, law-enforcement action and
  significant research.
- **Our own words, with sources.** Every item summarises the reporting and links to the original outlets, vendors and
  government advisories.
- **Something to act on.** Where there's a fix or a check to run, the item says so.
- **No speculation.** Attribution comes only from the sources ("X says group Y was responsible"). Ransomware
  leak-site claims are reported only once they're corroborated, and only the victim and the group are named.

## How the Intel reports work

Intel reports look across the archive of daily briefs for what keeps happening: which groups, vendors, sectors and
flaws recur, what rose or fell since the previous period, and what to watch next.

- Every finding cites the brief dates it rests on, e.g. "(16 Jul, 22 Jul)".
- Every count is computed from the tagged briefs, not estimated, and counts distinct brief days.
- Patterns seen fewer than three times are labelled as thin.
